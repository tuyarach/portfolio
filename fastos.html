FastOS · Product design

Making an AI manufacturing workflow easier to understand and manage

I restructured a fragmented process management experience so engineers could see how process items, process steps, and work elements connect.

Role
Lead Designer
Timeline
2 weeks
Team
1 designer, 1 engineer
Methods
User research, UX design, accessibility

Overview

Engineers were managing one process across three disconnected views

Manufacturing processes have three levels: process items, process steps, and work elements. The original interface separated each level into its own tab, making their relationships hard to understand and making edits harder to evaluate in context.

The core design question became: how might we show the connection between all three levels while giving engineers enough context to review them at a glance?

44related support tickets reviewed
3connected levels that needed one coherent view

Research

Four interviews revealed how engineers think about process information

I combined interviews with manufacturing staff, support-ticket analysis, and a review of competing products. Four insights shaped the design:

  1. Changes create ripple effects. Engineers need to see how an edit to one object affects the rest of a process.
  2. Most processes are already established. Engineers usually know the available processes and focus on reviewing or making targeted edits.
  3. AI output needs context. Skepticism grows when extracted information cannot be reviewed in its full sequence.
  4. The page supports review more than creation. The interface needed to make scanning and finding information fast.

Design direction

Balancing complete information with manageable cognitive load

I compared two directions before moving into the final interaction model.

Comparison of early design directions
DirectionStrengthTradeoff
Card viewShows complete AI-extracted information for review.Creates high cognitive load when processes contain many steps and elements.
Condensed tableMakes relationships easier to scan and navigate.Requires details to be progressively disclosed rather than shown all at once.

The research favored a condensed sequential view. Engineers already knew most process content, so the interface could prioritize hierarchy, relationships, and quick access to details.

Solution

An interconnected interface that preserves process context

01

Relationships become visible through selection

Selecting a process item filters its related process steps; selecting a step then filters the corresponding work elements. The cascade reinforces how the three levels connect.

02

Search keeps results in sequence

Search highlights an item, step, or element while preserving its exact place in the workflow. Engineers can find information quickly without losing the context needed to judge ripple effects.

Impact

The shipped design cut weekly support requests in half for the measured customer group

After release, process-page support requests from three customers fell from two per week to one per week. The result suggested that making the hierarchy visible reduced the need for founder-led explanation and support.

50%reduction in related weekly support tickets

What I learned

Limited time and startup resources required a pragmatic research approach. Interviews with engineering and manufacturing contacts, existing support conversations, and competitor patterns created enough evidence to make a focused decision and ship it.
